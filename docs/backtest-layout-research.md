# Redesigning the daily backtest

## What the current screen asks of a new user

A person opening Backtest sees three clickable strategy names inside a table of returns. Those buttons change the simulated entry rule, the account history, the chart markers and the context sent to the assistant. Their labels give no clue about most of those effects. The chart draws all four averages even when only two determine the selected strategy. The date picker opens on the last session, which can have no signal. The first visible invitation to investigate is a blank chat box. Dates in the ledger farther down the page are the route to a specific decision. ## Patterns from platform documentation

| Platform | Documented interaction | Adaptation for Arbitrade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TrendSpider | Defines conditions for entering and exiting before a run. Its results can be explained using a contextual AI action. [Strategy setup](https://help.trendspider.com/articles/accessing-and-using-the-strategy-tester) · [AI Explain](https://help.trendspider.com/kb/strategy-tester/strategy-tester-faq-markdown-html) | Write the shared entry and exit rule once. Give the optional buying condition its own labeled setting. Open the assistant on a selected signal with a question already in context. |
| TradingView | The Metrics view summarizes performance. A trade's entry or exit can take the user to its bar in the standard strategy report. [Strategy report and trades](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/strategies/#strategy-tester) | Give each signal a visible inspection action. Include skipped entries, which have no fill and would disappear from a trades-only list. |
| QuantConnect | The results page shows strategy equity and drawdown. Opening an order reveals its events. [Backtest results](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/cloud-platform/backtesting/results) | Lead with what happened to the simulated account. Put the candle chart, order status and fill time under the specific signal. |

These are documented workflows. The assessment of how they would help Arbitrade is a design hypothesis for a first-use test.

## Changes in implementation order

1. **Name the base rule and expose one choice.** Use “Daily moving-average crossover” with the plain-language entry and exit. Label the selector “Extra condition for buying.” Default to “None — crossover only.” The alternatives are “Close above the 200-day simple average” and “Close above the 200-day exponential average.” Explain on selection that the simple average weights closes equally and the exponential average gives more weight to recent closes. A failed filter skips that buy opportunity. Exits follow the same downward crossover for all variants.
2. **Give the page one starting action.** Open the completed baseline on an Overview showing AAPL, daily bars, tested dates, starting and ending account value, an equity curve, and largest peak-to-trough decline. Offer “Inspect a signal” as the primary action, initially jumping to the most recent eligible crossover. Put the existing three-row performance table behind “Compare entry filters.” In comparison, offer “Review a changed signal” and select the same date in both variants. If none changed, say so. Put the one-share sizing and next-open fill assumption next to the return. Keep detailed cash, positions, realized/unrealized P&L, ledger and assumptions accessible.
3. **Make the signal the main detail view.** Lead with a sentence explaining what happened. Show the 20/50 pair and only the selected filter line. Add Previous signal and Next signal with a secondary date picker. Selecting a marker or a ledger row should open the same record and retain its active filter. Show the action at the decision close, then any resulting order and subsequent fill in a distinct row. On phones this becomes one column in that reading order.
4. **Attach AI review to that record.** Render the deterministic explanation locally so the first interaction works offline. “Ask about this signal” opens the chat for follow-ups. “Get AI assessment” requests a fresh model opinion. Show model action, agreement or disagreement, and model-returned probability when available. Explain that probability describes the returned choice, distinct from a measured historical win rate. Expose raw tool results on expansion. A failed call displays “Assessment unavailable” alongside the original rule decision.
5. **Measure future AI participation as a separate paper experiment.** Start with an explicit policy such as “AI may veto an eligible entry.” Preserve the rules-only baseline. Freeze the policy, model and prompt versions, data, session cutoff and position for each decision. Exclude later fills and full-period returns from the AI inputs. Persist each returned assessment, timeout policy and simulated action. Recalculate cash and positions in the AI-assisted account as soon as its actions diverge. Compare under identical execution assumptions on a reserved later period or forward paper run. A retrospective model may already know historical events. Forward paper observation is especially valuable. Paper execution remains a separate deliberate workflow.

## First-use check

Give someone the workstation with no walkthrough. Ask “What makes this strategy buy?”, “Find an entry the simple-average filter skipped” and “If the AI disagrees, which action affected the simulated balance?” Watch their first click, whether they find the signal from the overview, and whether they distinguish the model assessment from a fill. A passing walkthrough ends with the user locating June 6, comparing the two rule actions on that date and identifying the next-open fill only in the unfiltered run. Repeat at phone width. This usability test is proposed.

On June 6, 2025 at 20:00 UTC, AAPL closed at $203.92. The upward crossover triggered a baseline buy, filled June 9 at $204.39. Both filters skipped the entry. The simple average was $225.12 and the exponential average was $215.59. These existing records supply a test case for the redesign.
